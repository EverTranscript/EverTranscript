# 09: M3 close-out — the measured DER, the bake-off, the guarantees

**What to build:** The evidence that Diarization works, on both platforms, measured rather than asserted. M1's close-out owed a WER; M2's owed false-negative rates; this one owes a **Diarization Error Rate**.

**Blocked by:** 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08.

Status: closed, and the real-meeting number is poor. On the AMI corpus's 16-meeting test set **DER is 49.7%**, where pyannote's own pipeline publishes 18.8% with the same two models. The measurement locates the gap, turn placement first and the embedding second, and the bake-off shows ReDimNet removes most of the embedding's share. Neither is fixed here; what to do is escalated (`DECISIONS.md` Q112). The 3.9% below is a two-voice construction, not a meeting, and what each sample is *not* is stated beside it.

- [x] **DER = 3.9%** on a labelled two-speaker construction: 60 s, four 15 s turns A-B-A-B, reference speech 40.6 s by an energy VAD. Missed 1.6 s, confused 0.0 s, two speakers found for two. Solo case on the real recording: one speaker found for one — the failure that matters there is inventing a second, and it did not.

  **The measurement found three defects, which is the entire reason for owing a number.** Each was invisible to every unit test, and each is now one:
  1. **Turn coverage was conflated with embedding span.** The catalog's span rules clip to the middle 10 s and drop anything under 1.5 s — correct for choosing what to build a Voiceprint from, and disastrous as a definition of who was talking. 28% of speech had no speaker. `spans` and `embeddable` are separate now. DER 38.4% → 26.6%.
  2. **One embedding per contiguous span made two people one speaker.** Two voices alternating without a pause between them is one span, one vector, one speaker. Confusion 23.6%. Embedding in 3 s sub-windows is what lets clustering see the change. DER 26.6% → 3.9%, confusion → 0.0%.
  3. **`agglomerate` was not agglomerative.** A single pass joining each cluster to the first earlier one within threshold split one voice into two groups that were each internally consistent and never compared — three speakers in a two-speaker recording, both extras the same person. Closest-pair-first against a running centroid fixed it.

  **How to re-run it:** the harness was scratch, never committed (`derharness`), and was deleted with the session scratchpad on 2026-09-15. Inputs are derived from the M1 dogfood recording: mic channel at 16 kHz as voice A, the same passage resampled down as voice B, four 15 s turns concatenated.
- [x] **DER 49.7% on real meetings**, measured 2026-09-15 on the AMI Meeting Corpus test set: 16 meetings, 9.1 hours, about four speakers each, Mix-Headset audio. Scored against BUT's `only_words` reference with no collar and overlapped speech included, which is the protocol pyannote publishes under. Missed 28.0%, false alarm 2.7%, confusion 18.9%; by meeting, from 19.1% (IS1009c) to 72.7% (EN2002b). **pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1 publishes 18.8% under the same protocol with the same segmentation and embedding models** (missed 9.5%, confusion 5.7%), so the gap is this pipeline's and not the models'. The number is the shipped `LiveDiarizer`'s; the copy instrumented for the analysis below produced the same turns on all 16 meetings.

  **Where the points go.** An oracle gives every window this pipeline embeds the reference speaker who covers most of it. That is perfect clustering, and it scores 32.6%.
  - **Turn placement costs 32.6 points, and no embedding or threshold can win them back.** It is defect 1's family again: a rule about what a Voiceprint is built from, deciding who was talking. `spans` ends a turn at any frame where segmentation hears two voices, so overlap longer than the 400 ms merge gap gets no turn at all. The module note says overlap gets one turn, attributed to whichever voice it resembles; the code does not do that. 27.0% of this set's speaker-time is overlapped. Turns are also made only from embedded sub-windows, which need 1.5 s, so a shorter stretch gets no speaker, and single-speaker stretches under 1.5 s are 6.3% of speaker-time. pyannote misses 9.5% with the same segmentation model.
  - **Clustering costs the other 17.1 points, and they are the embedding's.** ReDimNet on the same windows leaves about 3 (the bake-off, next box).
  - **Clustering time is cubic in meeting length.** A ceiling rather than a DER finding: `agglomerate` took 2.5 s on a 17.5-minute meeting (411 windows), 36 s on 34 minutes (1,012 windows) and 70 s on 50 minutes (1,259 windows), in a release build on an M2. A two-hour meeting would spend roughly a quarter of an hour clustering.

  Clusters holding at least `MIN_SPEAKER_MS` of turns, the ones that would be minted: 2.9 a meeting against 3.94 real speakers, out of 10 to 59 clusters a meeting.

  **What the sample is not.** Mix-Headset sums each participant's close-talking headset, so it is cleaner than a laptop microphone across a room, and a far-field DER would be worse. It is one in-room channel, where a call would split the Operator from everyone else. And it is not EverTranscript's own capture. Original criterion: M2 ended with six defects of one family: the code believed something the machine did not honor, and every test agreed because the tests shared the belief. The M3 form of that is a pipeline that scores well on curated audio and falls apart on a real meeting. **Fixture audio is evidence about fixtures**

  WeSpeaker leaves 17 to 24 points of clustering error, ReDimNet about 3. WeSpeaker's dev curve is flat from 0.40 to 0.60 (52.5 to 53.3) while test moves from 57.7 to 49.7 over the same range, so dev cannot choose its threshold: 0.6 stays, and the fragility is the finding. Both ReDimNets bottom out on dev around 0.55 to 0.60, and test at dev's pick is within 1.5 points of test's best. Times are the whole pipeline on an idle M2.

  **Recognition across meetings**, which is what a bake-off was said to buy. A voiceprint is a duration-weighted average of one speaker's windows in one meeting: the windows the reference says they hold at least 90% of, 10 s or more in all. It is scored against the other meetings in its series, where the other voices are the same colleagues in the same room.

  | Embedding | Test EER | Test: nearest voice is right | Dev EER | Dev: nearest voice is right | Different colleagues above `MATCH_FLOOR`, test / dev |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | WeSpeaker | 10.3% | 97.7% | 13.3% | 92.8% | 15.3% / 44.0% |
  | ReDimNet-B2 | 0.0% | 100% | 0.0% | 100% | 0% / 0% |
  | ReDimNet2-B3 | 0.0% | 100% | 0.0% | 100% | 0% / 0% |

  That is 87 same-person pairs, 261 different-person pairs and 174 nearest-voice trials on test; 69, 207 and 138 on dev. With WeSpeaker, the floor is not what stops a colleague getting someone else's name; `MATCH_MARGIN` and mutual agreement are. These voiceprints are cleaner than the product's, which come from its own clusters, so the product's rates are likely worse. Across series, a few dev pairs of one person in two IB meetings score low under every model (WeSpeaker's lowest is 0.011), which is probably the corpus's labels; not checked.

  **Prior art:** Granola 7.515.1 downloads ReDimNet2-B3 as ONNX for exactly the cross-meeting job, and clusters with a WeSpeaker-family embedding and PLDA inside its native diarizer.

  **Not adopted here.** Old and new vectors cannot be compared: `cosine` scores mismatched lengths 0, so every existing Speaker would silently stop being recognized. Neither ReDimNet is published as ONNX, so an export needs a host that CI and the app can fetch from. And `MATCH_FLOOR` and `MATCH_MARGIN` need deriving again, the way the merge threshold was here. Escalated as `DECISIONS.md` Q112.

  **How to re-run it:** rebuild the scratch, which was deleted on 2026-09-15 and never committed: the harness; a Python copy of `agglomerate`, which reproduced the Rust output exactly for WeSpeaker and ReDimNet-B2 on all 16 test meetings (and on spot checks for ReDimNet2-B3), which is what makes the sweep the product's clusterer; and the sweep, oracle and recognition scripts. Data: AMI Mix-Headset (CC BY 4.0) and BUT's AMI-diarization-setup references (Apache-2.0), scored with pyannote.metrics. Both ReDimNets come from their torch.hub releases, exported with torch's dynamo ONNX exporter at opset 18, and match PyTorch at cosine above 0.9999.

  **An interaction worth recording:** re-diarizing *after* deleting a Voiceprint creates a new Speaker and leaves the old one with zero appearances, because re-diarization replaces the machine's attribution and the old Speaker can no longer be recognized. ADR-0009 makes Speaker records permanent, so this is not something to fix by adding a delete; it is something an ADR should decide.
