# Diarization follows the pyannote recipe; ReDimNet2-B3 is its one embedding, self-hosted; a model change re-runs History

> **Amended 2026-09-16 (the embedding clause is withdrawn; the rest stands):** this ADR was never on `main` — it was written on the unmerged branch `diarization-pyannote-redimnet2` while eleven citations to it landed in `main`'s source. It is recorded here so those citations resolve, with the one clause the measurement contradicts withdrawn. **The recipe stands and has shipped**: segmentation keeps which local speaker holds each frame, each is embedded with the others masked out, and overlap produces a turn per speaker. **The model-change policy stands as policy but has *not* shipped** — the correction is in the second amendment below. What shipped is the persistent queue it would run on (DECISIONS Q133, ticket 08). The wipe migration and the bulk re-run did not land here; a model change is currently handled by a different and narrower mechanism, which is not the same promise. **The choice of ReDimNet2-B3 does not stand as argued.** Its stated ground — "0% cross-meeting EER where WeSpeaker put 15–44% of colleagues above the match floor" — came from a bake-off that ran all three candidates through a front end correct for only one of them (Q111, diagnosed in Q115), so it measured our own feature extraction rather than the models. **Neither acceptance bar this ADR set is usable as written.** AMI test DER ≤ 18.8% sits below the oracle floor this pipeline measures, about 19.5%. That floor is conditional on the hypothesis spans actually scored: `oracle_relabel` relabels those spans, so missed speech and false alarm survive it and only labelling error is removed. What it licenses is exactly one sentence — **these fixed spans cannot reach 18.8% by oracle relabelling alone** — and nothing about which stage would have to change, nor that the bar is unreachable in principle. Cross-meeting EER ≤ 1% was derived from the broken bake-off, so it is unsupported as a bar; that is a statement about its derivation and not evidence that ≤ 1% is unreachable. Which model ships is escalated and open (Q139, superseding Q137); until it is answered WeSpeaker stays, being what is in the build. The thresholds this ADR left as placeholders are still placeholders: Granola's 0.45 / 0.10, confirmed by reading its bundle on 2026-09-16, against our shipped 0.62 / 0.08.
>
> **Amended 2026-09-16, second pass (the all-pairs recognition column is withdrawn; measured evidence below).** The first amendment reported a recognition comparison — "nearest voice right 43.5% against 39.0% and cross-meeting EER 37.74% against 39.18%" — that is **withdrawn as confounded** (Q140). Both metrics are computed over all pairs of clusters, so the number of trials moves with the cluster count: measured across the merge sweep, nearest-voice-right rises monotonically from 33.3% at 0.30 to 72.4% at 0.90 while DER over the same range goes from 37% to 86%, so the metric improves exactly where the partition is getting worse. Fragmentation is the available explanation for that co-movement; it is not established as the mechanism by it. Either way the trial count is not a constant of the corpus but a function of the partition, so two arms that fragment differently are not being asked the same question — which is the reason these numbers are withdrawn rather than restated. It is withdrawn from this ADR rather than restated, and the enrollment replay below is what replaced it.
>
> *Measurements.* All on the committed harness, AMI dev (18 meetings) and test (16, disjoint), the pipeline fixed except where stated.
>
> - **Unconstrained DER, each model at its own dev-selected merge threshold** (Q140, Q143): dev, WeSpeaker 29.13% at 0.65 against ReDimNet2 26.01% at 0.60, a 3.12-point gap; held-out test, 28.64% against 24.62%, a 4.02-point gap, all of it confusion — missed, false alarm and the oracle floor agree to within 0.35, 0.06 and 0.11. This is the gap for **the existing unconstrained path at per-model dev-selected thresholds**, which is not the shipped configuration: production runs one merge threshold of 0.60 for whatever model is loaded, and WeSpeaker was given 0.65 here so that each model was judged at its own best measured dev point. Nothing below retracts it.
> - **Chronological enrollment replay, shipped matcher** (Q145, as corrected by Q146): ReDimNet2 has less unattributed, more correct and more wrong returning time on both splits. Net changes in the returning buckets, dev: correct +311.390s, wrong +509.740s. Test: correct +993.600s, wrong +489.550s. These are net bucket differences between two runs and not measured transitions; nothing here establishes that particular abstaining seconds became particular correct or wrong ones. Newcomers improve on both splits with no bucket worsening. Denominators identical, zero `unattributed:unexpected`.
> - **A same-window cannot-link constraint** built only from segmentation window and channel provenance, harness-only (Q147, as corrected by Q150): at the **actually shipped** merge threshold of 0.60 it takes WeSpeaker's dev DER from 29.60% to 27.34%, 2.26 points, with nothing else changed; retuned to its best measured dev threshold of 0.10 it reaches 22.48%, 6.65 points below the unconstrained dev optimum, 6.29 of which is confusion. The oracle floor moves 0.11.
> - **Under the constraint the DER ranking reverses** (Q149): both models pinned at 0.10, one held-out run each, no sweep — WeSpeaker 23.51%, ReDimNet2 23.94%, the 0.43-point gap again entirely confusion. The constraint is worth 5.13 held-out points to WeSpeaker and 0.68 to ReDimNet2.
> - **Recognition under the constraint, at points declared before the split was looked at** (Q151, as corrected by Q152; Q153): the dev matcher grid's selections show **negative transfer** — each model's dev-selected point loses about 280s of correct returning against its own shipped point on held-out test, while improving the other three quantities. At the declared primary comparison, WeSpeaker 0.70/0.08 against ReDimNet2 0.80/0.08, ReDimNet2 is better on all four: correct returning 18998.020s against 16758.720s (+2239.300s, 8.77 points of the 25538.370s denominator), wrong returning 3906.650s against 4309.370s (−402.720s), correct-new 4960.354s against 4958.104s, newcomer false attachment zero against 2.250s. The same dominance holds at the shared shipped point and at 0.80/0.00; it does not hold at 0.80/0.15, which is a trade. **Both constrained configurations still have less correct and more wrong returning time than their own earlier unconstrained replay**, so the constraint's DER gain is not a recognition gain.
>
> *Mechanism hypotheses, not established by the above.* That the constraint puts a floor under the cluster count which roughly matches the truth, and so removes the system's sensitivity to a hand-tuned threshold, is a reading of the dev grid's flat 0.00–0.30 basin and its 112 clusters against 72 real speakers — not a measurement of cause. That most of what ReDimNet2 was buying is resistance to over-merging which the constraint now supplies structurally is likewise a reading of the 5.13-against-0.68 asymmetry and the dev crossover between 0.50 and 0.60. Blocking a merge changes later centroids and merge histories, so the net figures do not isolate what the constraint removed. Aggregate ledgers cannot say which abstaining seconds became correct or wrong ones; no paired per-segment transition was measured.
>
> - **The split-model architecture, on dev** (Q171, corrected by Q174 and Q176): **half measured; the verdict is withdrawn.** Eight cells — each embedding's partition crossed with each embedding's vectors, in both clustering arms, over the 32-point matcher grid — with turn placement held fixed so every cell's DER equals its clustering control's. The first grid ran every cell on the tracks *both* passes produced, and that restriction is a different experiment: dropping nine observations changes what WeSpeaker's clusterer sees, moving the wrong-returning bucket by a net −63.960s at constrained 0.62/0.08, with 212 per-event differences across that control grid — a difference between two runs' totals, not a measured transition. All nine are WeSpeaker-only, so ReDimNet2's tracks are a subset of WeSpeaker's and **the ReDimNet2-clustering row ran on its complete original inputs and stands** — with its results scoped to the configurations they hold at, which the first statement of them was not. Substituting WeSpeaker's identity vectors under ReDimNet2's partition costs at most 60s of correct returning time **at constrained 0.80/0.00**, but 1325.430s at 0.62/0.08 (16213.960s against 14888.530s) and 1086.060s at 0.80/0.15; the near-inertness is a property of that one well-calibrated point and is not a bound over the grid. The claim that survives unscoped is the null at **unconstrained 0.45/0.00**, where all four quantities are identical to the second under ReDimNet2's partition. **The WeSpeaker-clustering row is a labelled diagnostic**, including the grid's single dominance (constrained 0.30/0.00, where both controls are badly calibrated anyway); its 22.46% DER is the restricted partition's, against 22.48% original. The harness now uses a left join onto the clustering pass's own observations and turns, so nothing is dropped, padded or borrowed. What this does **not** establish is that no split pays: dominating both same-model controls is sufficient evidence of a recognition benefit and not necessary for a split to be worthwhile, since those four quantities are the recognition column alone and price neither DER nor the second inference pass. The measured cost of a split is two models in the bundle and two inference passes per meeting; it does **not** entail two persisted identity spaces per Speaker, because clustering vectors can stay meeting-local and never be stored against a Speaker at all. Held-out points (Q172) are **provisional** — calibrated from the withdrawn half. *Mechanism hypothesis, not measured:* the gallery is seeded from cluster centroids, so the matcher is asked about clusters the partition defined.
>
> *What is not measured.* Nine observations of 12,649 exist in only one pass — all WeSpeaker-only, all 84–85ms — because ReDimNet2's waveform front end yields no features where WeSpeaker's fbank does. Coverage is reported per model as that pass's own matched fraction; the 0.0012% figure previously given here double-counted the common tracks by summing both passes into its denominator, and is withdrawn.
>
> *Reserved to the user, and deliberately held open by them.* One: **whether ≥ 2.0 points of DER is the adoption bar** — asked, and left undecided on purpose, which is not the same as unanswered. The split-model question was released for measurement; half its dev grid stands and half is withdrawn (Q174), no product verdict follows from it (Q176), and adopting anything from it would still be the user's. Separately unresolved, and not a substitute for either: **what rate of exchange holds between a correct and a wrong attributed second**, without which the recognition column cannot be collapsed to a single ranking. Nothing above assigns any of the three, and no ranking of two outcomes that differ in more than one direction has been made here. Q152's rule applies to everything in this ADR: measurements, mechanism hypotheses and utility judgements stay separately labelled, and a sentence that ranks two outcomes is a utility judgement no matter how it is phrased.
>
> *The cannot-link constraint is harness-only* and production still calls the unconstrained `agglomerate`.
>
> *The model-change policy, as a policy.* This ADR's third paragraph — wipe every Voiceprint, re-run History oldest first, seed a named Speaker from its own attributed segments under the Operator's corrections — **stands and is not withdrawn.** It has not shipped. What `main` does today on a model change is narrower: each exemplar from another space is re-embedded from the sample window it kept, at the moment its Meeting is next diarized (Q115, ADR-0035 as amended). That rebuilds *evidence* and never re-runs *attribution*, and it uses the old model's choice of cuts, which is the thing this ADR rejected for a stated reason. Tickets 05 and 12 carry the policy and are rewritten against the current code rather than closed.
>
> *Model identity.* Independent of the choice, the identity a vector is stamped with now comes from the registry entry rather than a constant, follows the model that actually ran, and is checked before any seed is scored against it (Q154). This is what makes a second embedding safe to hold in the tree at all: before it, every vector the whole comparison produced was labelled WeSpeaker.

The shipped pipeline scored 49.7% DER on AMI's test set where pyannote publishes 18.8% with the same two models, and about a third of the gap was our own turn placement (DECISIONS Q111, Q112). Decided 2026-09-15, interviewed against Granola 7.515.1's live worker and Swift sidecar and anarlog's `pyannote-local` (`docs/competitive-facts-2026-08-27.md`, corrected the same day): Diarization implements the **pyannote community-1 recipe** that both of Granola's pipelines run — 10 s segmentation windows slid at 1 s, one embedding per local speaker per window, agglomerative clustering, per-speaker reconstruction with overlapping turns and no duration floor — as the same post-meeting Core job on both platforms. The embedding for **both clustering and Voiceprints is ReDimNet2-B3** (PalabraAI, MIT, 192-d, raw 16 kHz waveform in, mel frontend inside the graph), replacing WeSpeaker ResNet34-LM; plain agglomerative clustering, no PLDA. Granola splits the two jobs across a masked WeSpeaker export and ReDimNet2-B3; we take the one model because it measured 0% cross-meeting EER where WeSpeaker put 15–44% of colleagues above the match floor, and because no published ONNX carries the speaker-mask input the masked path needs, so either design ships a self-made export. The export is ours: a committed script under `uv run` with pinned torch, published to a Hugging Face org the product controls — the same host as every other Provisioned Model, so Sanctioned Traffic (ADR-0034) is unchanged. The standard is two measured bars on a committed harness: **AMI test DER ≤ 18.8%** on the shipped path, and **cross-meeting recognition EER ≤ 1%** with no different-colleague pair above the floor.

**A model change discards every Voiceprint and re-runs every Meeting with Kept Audio.** Old and new vectors cannot be compared, and ADR-0035 promised a clean re-embed from kept audio. The upgrade migration nulls every Voiceprint and deletes every exemplar, keeping every Speaker, name and attribution; seeding filters by the current model name as a standing guard. A bulk re-run then walks History oldest first, one persistent queue that a just-ended Meeting or an Operator request enters at the front, pausing while any Meeting records, resuming across restarts, visible and cancellable in the Registry. In each re-run Meeting a *named* Speaker is seeded from its own attributed segments embedded by the new model — the Operator's word about the voice, which ADR-0009 already puts outside the machine's reach — and those windows become its new exemplars. Pseudonymous Speakers are re-minted, so their numbers change. The Operator's Voiceprint is rebuilt by the channel rules alone (ADR-0029 as amended 2026-09-15), never from old attributions. A Meeting without Kept Audio keeps the old run's attributions.

## Considered options

Keep WeSpeaker and add PLDA/VBx (17 to 24 points of clustering error stay); Granola's two-model split (two self-hosted exports, and the masked path is unmeasured here); ReDimNet-B2 (about a point behind B3, no other difference). For the migration: re-embed only the old exemplars' stored sample offsets (rejected: uses the old model's cuts rather than the Operator's confirmation of whole clusters); a wipe with no re-run (rejected: a History nobody is recognized in); keep both vector spaces (rejected: recognition would silently depend on which model heard whom).

## Consequences

- Every installed build pins the export's URL and checksum; publishing it is an outward-facing act that happens once per model, under a written decision, never as a side effect of a build.
- Existing Voiceprints are gone on upgrade. Named Speakers come back as their Meetings are re-run; a named Speaker whose every Meeting lost its audio ends with no Voiceprint and a Registry note saying why.
- The M3 catalog's "reject, don't queue" for batch compute becomes "one queue, the fresh Meeting at the front"; only a Meeting already in line is refused.
- Thresholds (merge, match floor, margin) are re-derived on AMI dev and reported on test through the harness; until then Granola's 0.45 / 0.10 and the bake-off's 0.60 stand as named placeholders.
