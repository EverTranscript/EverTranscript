# Diarization follows the pyannote recipe; ReDimNet2-B3 is its one embedding, self-hosted; a model change re-runs History

> **Amended 2026-09-17, third of the day (the matcher point is selected and the DER bar is answered):** the user delegated both reserved decisions rather than assigning numbers — the reply was *"do 1,2,3"* — so the paragraph below that calls them open is superseded for both. **The matcher point stays 0.62/0.08**, selected from the three points predeclared in Q182 and run in Q183, read only in the arm that ships (unconstrained, ReDimNet2 clustering and identity, held-out AMI test). No new run, no retuning. 0.30/0.00 is dominated on all four quantities. Against 0.80/0.00 the shipped point is a trade — correct returning +303.790s for wrong returning +37.700s, newcomers tied at 4677.384s correct and zero false attachment — so it is decided by the rate of exchange, now assumed explicitly at **4 seconds of correct returning per 1 second of wrong**, which respects M3's rule that a wrong attribution costs more than an unnamed one. The break-even is 8.06, so the shipped point wins for any rate below that and the choice does not turn on the exact figure; a reader who holds a wrong second to be worth more than eight correct ones should overrule it to 0.80/0.00. `MATCH_FLOOR` and `MATCH_MARGIN` are unchanged, now by selection rather than inheritance (Q231). **The ≥ 2.0-point DER bar is not adopted, and the empty field is closed rather than filled** (Q232): a single DER number is the wrong instrument on this record, because the same-window constraint reverses the DER ranking between the two models while recognition does not move with it (Q149, Q153), so a bar on DER alone can be satisfied by a configuration that recognises people worse. What stands in its place is a reporting requirement for any future embedding change, stated there.

A fresh install runs the same sequence and walks nothing. Still unchosen: the matcher thresholds (ticket 07) and the DER bar.

> **Amended 2026-09-17 (the embedding is adopted; the ground is the measured DER, not the withdrawn EER):** ReDimNet2-B3 ships as the one embedding, decided by the user on the record the two amendments below set out — unconstrained DER at each model's own dev-selected merge threshold, 26.01% against WeSpeaker's 29.13% on AMI dev and 24.62% against 28.64% held-out, all of the held-out gap confusion (DECISIONS Q140, Q143); recognition at the declared primary matcher points dominant on all four quantities (Q153). Not on the ground this ADR first gave, which stays withdrawn. The registry entry is the one that changed: `redimnet2-b3-vox2-lm`, our own export of `PalabraAI/redimnet2` v1.0.0 (MIT) published unchanged to a Hugging Face org this product controls, stamped `redimnet2-b3` version `1` — the identity every vector the A/B harness produced from it already carried. The front end now rides on the identity, so the model and its input are named in one place. **What does not change with it:** the same-window constraint stays harness-only, having a measured DER cost at the shipped threshold and a measured recognition cost at every point (Q151, Q214 as corrected by Q216); the matcher thresholds stay where they are, ticket 07's rate of exchange still unchosen; the DER adoption bar stays an empty field; and the model-change wipe and re-run stay unregistered — the user's word is "register the migrations later". **What the interval before registration does, corrected the same day (Q227):** it is not the case that every returning Speaker is a stranger. An installed History holds 256-wide WeSpeaker exemplars under the old stamp, and the first ordinary Diarization after upgrade runs the lazy path this ADR rejected: `stale_exemplars` selects every one of them, `runner::rebuild` re-embeds each from the sample window *WeSpeaker* chose, and `adopt_rebuilt` files the ReDimNet2 vector under the new stamp and recomputes the Voiceprint, in that Meeting's own transaction — every stale row on every Speaker, not only that Meeting's. Recognition therefore continues across the change, on evidence of the kind the third paragraph of this ADR rejected: the old model's cuts, not the Operator's whole attributed clusters. A Speaker whose exemplars have no window or whose Meeting is gone loses its Voiceprint under it. Registration then discards all of it and earns the Voiceprints back from attribution. Whether the lazy path should be closed before registration is the user's call; nothing here changes it.

Cross-meeting EER ≤ 1% was derived from the broken bake-off, so it is unsupported as a bar; that is a statement about its derivation and not evidence that ≤ 1% is unreachable. > **Amended 2026-09-16, second pass (the all-pairs recognition column is withdrawn; measured evidence below).** The first amendment reported a recognition comparison — "nearest voice right 43.5% against 39.0% and cross-meeting EER 37.74% against 39.18%" — that is **withdrawn as confounded** (Q140). Both metrics are computed over all pairs of clusters, so the number of trials moves with the cluster count: measured across the merge sweep, nearest-voice-right rises monotonically from 33.3% at 0.30 to 72.4% at 0.90 while DER over the same range goes from 37% to 86%, so the metric improves exactly where the partition is getting worse. Fragmentation is the available explanation for that co-movement; it is not established as the mechanism by it. Either way the trial count is not a constant of the corpus but a function of the partition, so two arms that fragment differently are not being asked the same question — which is the reason these numbers are withdrawn rather than restated. It is withdrawn from this ADR rather than restated, and the enrollment replay below is what replaced it.

> *Measurements.* All on the committed harness, AMI dev (18 meetings) and test (16, disjoint), the pipeline fixed except where stated.
>
> - **Unconstrained DER, each model at its own dev-selected merge threshold** (Q140, Q143): dev, WeSpeaker 29.13% at 0.65 against ReDimNet2 26.01% at 0.60, a 3.12-point gap; held-out test, 28.64% against 24.62%, a 4.02-point gap, all of it confusion — missed, false alarm and the oracle floor agree to within 0.35, 0.06 and 0.11. This is the gap for **the existing unconstrained path at per-model dev-selected thresholds**, which is not the shipped configuration: production runs one merge threshold of 0.60 for whatever model is loaded, and WeSpeaker was given 0.65 here so that each model was judged at its own best measured dev point. Nothing below retracts it.
> - **Chronological enrollment replay, shipped matcher** (Q145, as corrected by Q146): ReDimNet2 has less unattributed, more correct and more wrong returning time on both splits. Net changes in the returning buckets, dev: correct +311.390s, wrong +509.740s. Test: correct +993.600s, wrong +489.550s. These are net bucket differences between two runs and not measured transitions; nothing here establishes that particular abstaining seconds became particular correct or wrong ones. Newcomers improve on both splits with no bucket worsening. Denominators identical, zero `unattributed:unexpected`.
> - **A same-window cannot-link constraint** built only from segmentation window and channel provenance, harness-only (Q147, as corrected by Q150): at the **actually shipped** merge threshold of 0.60 it takes WeSpeaker's dev DER from 29.60% to 27.34%, 2.26 points, with nothing else changed; retuned to its best measured dev threshold of 0.10 it reaches 22.48%, 6.65 points below the unconstrained dev optimum, 6.29 of which is confusion. The oracle floor moves 0.11.
> - **Under the constraint the DER ranking reverses** (Q149): both models pinned at 0.10, one held-out run each, no sweep — WeSpeaker 23.51%, ReDimNet2 23.94%, the 0.43-point gap again entirely confusion. The constraint is worth 5.13 held-out points to WeSpeaker and 0.68 to ReDimNet2.
> - **Recognition under the constraint, at points declared before the split was looked at** (Q151, as corrected by Q152; Q153): the dev matcher grid's selections show **negative transfer** — each model's dev-selected point loses about 280s of correct returning against its own shipped point on held-out test, while improving the other three quantities. At the declared primary comparison, WeSpeaker 0.70/0.08 against ReDimNet2 0.80/0.08, ReDimNet2 is better on all four: correct returning 18998.020s against 16758.720s (+2239.300s, 8.77 points of the 25538.370s denominator), wrong returning 3906.650s against 4309.370s (−402.720s), correct-new 4960.354s against 4958.104s, newcomer false attachment zero against 2.250s. The same dominance holds at the shared shipped point and at 0.80/0.00; it does not hold at 0.80/0.15, which is a trade. **Both constrained configurations still have less correct and more wrong returning time than their own earlier unconstrained replay**, so the constraint's DER gain is not a recognition gain.
>
> *Mechanism hypotheses, not established by the above.* That the constraint puts a floor under the cluster count which roughly matches the truth, and so removes the system's sensitivity to a hand-tuned threshold, is a reading of the dev grid's flat 0.00–0.30 basin and its 112 clusters against 72 real speakers — not a measurement of cause. That most of what ReDimNet2 was buying is resistance to over-merging which the constraint now supplies structurally is likewise a reading of the 5.13-against-0.68 asymmetry and the dev crossover between 0.50 and 0.60. Blocking a merge changes later centroids and merge histories, so the net figures do not isolate what the constraint removed. Aggregate ledgers cannot say which abstaining seconds became correct or wrong ones; no paired per-segment transition was measured.

> - **The split-model architecture, on dev** (Q180, superseding Q171): measured on complete inputs — each cell a left join of identity vectors onto its clustering pass's own observations and turns, after an earlier shared-track restriction was found to have changed WeSpeaker's constrained partition (Q174). Both diagonals now reproduce the standalone single-model ledgers event for event, denominators are fixed at 22182.515s returning and 7816.070s new across all eight cells, `unattributed:unexpected` is zero, and all four DER millisecond tallies are equal within each clustering row. **The identity embedding is not inert.** At the shipped matcher point, constrained 0.62/0.08, substituting ReDimNet2's identity vectors under WeSpeaker's partition moves correct returning +1976.310s and wrong returning +128.000s (correct-new −53.750s, false attachment +53.750s) — a trade reported with both numbers and not ranked, since the rate of exchange is unchosen and M3 holds a wrong attribution to cost more than an unnamed one. **One dominance over both same-model controls** exists in 128 comparisons, constrained 0.30/0.00 — but WeSpeaker alone at constrained 0.80/0.00 dominates it on three of four quantities and ties the fourth, so it does not establish an architectural advantage over calibrating one model. **An exact null** holds under ReDimNet2's unconstrained partition at four points, where substituting WeSpeaker's vectors changes nothing to the second; under the constraint the same swap costs 1325.430s at 0.62/0.08. Cost is harness timing: 583s + 606s of inference over 18 meetings, plus 408s of downstream replay the architecture would not repeat, and a production split could share one segmentation pass. A split does not entail two persisted identity spaces per Speaker. **Held-out AMI test, 24 predeclared configurations** (Q183): the low-floor diagnostic's dev dominance did **not** replicate — constrained 0.30/0.00 We-clustering/Re-identity is a trade against its own control and dominated by ReDimNet2's — and **none** of the twelve splits dominates both same-model controls of its arm and point. At the shipped matcher point the shape of the result changed rather than replicating: constrained 0.62/0.08, under the best DER measured (23.51%), substituting ReDimNet2's identity vectors dominates its own-partition control on all four (+1076.380s correct, −219.310s wrong, +13.330s correct-new, −13.330s false attachment) at identical DER, where on dev the same cell was a trade (+1976.310s correct, +128.000s wrong) — so no dominance replicated there either; what carried across is only the direction of correct returning. It still only trades against the ReDimNet2 control. The exact null replicated at unconstrained 0.80/0.00. At constrained 0.80/0.00 the two splits go opposite ways, so "the split" is not the subject: **We-clustering/Re-identity** is dominated by both controls (wrong +141.500s against We/We with the other three identical; correct −731.300s / wrong +338.520s against Re/Re), while **Re-clustering/We-identity** dominates We/We (correct +687.520s, wrong −283.880s) and trades against Re/Re, giving up 43.780s of correct returning for 86.860s less wrong. Coverage is reported per model as that pass's own matched fraction; the 0.0012% figure previously given here double-counted the common tracks by summing both passes into its denominator, and is withdrawn.

> *Reserved to the user, and deliberately held open by them.* One: **whether ≥ 2.0 points of DER is the adoption bar** — asked, and left undecided on purpose, which is not the same as unanswered, and not a premise anything else waits on: choosing a recognition threshold does not logically require a number for the bar. The open product choices are which model and configuration to adopt and which recognition outcome to prioritise; the bar is a deliberately empty field, not a mandatory one. The split-model question was released for measurement; half its dev grid stands and half is withdrawn (Q174), no product verdict follows from it (Q176), and adopting anything from it would still be the user's. Separately unresolved, and not a substitute for either: **what rate of exchange holds between a correct and a wrong attributed second**, without which the recognition column cannot be collapsed to a single ranking. Nothing above assigns any of the three, and no ranking of two outcomes that differ in more than one direction has been made here. Q152's rule applies to everything in this ADR: measurements, mechanism hypotheses and utility judgements stay separately labelled, and a sentence that ranks two outcomes is a utility judgement no matter how it is phrased.

> *The cannot-link constraint is harness-only* and production still calls the unconstrained `agglomerate`.
>
> *The model-change policy, as a policy.* This ADR's third paragraph — wipe every Voiceprint, re-run History oldest first, seed a named Speaker from its own attributed segments under the Operator's corrections — **stands and is not withdrawn.** It has not shipped. What `main` does today on a model change is narrower: each exemplar from another space is re-embedded from the sample window it kept, at the moment its Meeting is next diarized (Q115, ADR-0035 as amended). That rebuilds *evidence* and never re-runs *attribution*, and it uses the old model's choice of cuts, which is the thing this ADR rejected for a stated reason. Tickets 05 and 12 carry the policy and are rewritten against the current code rather than closed.
>
> *Model identity.* Independent of the choice, the identity a vector is stamped with now comes from the registry entry rather than a constant, follows the model that actually ran, and is checked before any seed is scored against it (Q154). This is what makes a second embedding safe to hold in the tree at all: before it, every vector the whole comparison produced was labelled WeSpeaker.

The shipped pipeline scored 49.7% DER on AMI's test set where pyannote publishes 18.8% with the same two models, and about a third of the gap was our own turn placement (DECISIONS Q111, Q112). Decided 2026-09-15, interviewed against Granola 7.515.1's live worker and Swift sidecar and anarlog's `pyannote-local` (`docs/competitive-facts-2026-08-27.md`, corrected the same day): Diarization implements the **pyannote community-1 recipe** that both of Granola's pipelines run — 10 s segmentation windows slid at 1 s, one embedding per local speaker per window, agglomerative clustering, per-speaker reconstruction with overlapping turns and no duration floor — as the same post-meeting Core job on both platforms. The embedding for **both clustering and Voiceprints is ReDimNet2-B3** (PalabraAI, MIT, 192-d, raw 16 kHz waveform in, mel frontend inside the graph), replacing WeSpeaker ResNet34-LM; plain agglomerative clustering, no PLDA. Granola splits the two jobs across a masked WeSpeaker export and ReDimNet2-B3; we take the one model because it measured 0% cross-meeting EER where WeSpeaker put 15–44% of colleagues above the match floor, and because no published ONNX carries the speaker-mask input the masked path needs, so either design ships a self-made export. The export is ours: a committed script under `uv run` with pinned torch, published to a Hugging Face org the product controls — the same host as every other Provisioned Model, so Sanctioned Traffic (ADR-0034) is unchanged. The standard is two measured bars on a committed harness: **AMI test DER ≤ 18.8%** on the shipped path, and **cross-meeting recognition EER ≤ 1%** with no different-colleague pair above the floor.

**A model change discards every Voiceprint and re-runs every Meeting with Kept Audio.** Old and new vectors cannot be compared, and ADR-0035 promised a clean re-embed from kept audio. The upgrade migration nulls every Voiceprint and deletes every exemplar, keeping every Speaker, name and attribution; seeding filters by the current model name as a standing guard. A bulk re-run then walks History oldest first, one persistent queue that a just-ended Meeting or an Operator request enters at the front, pausing while any Meeting records, resuming across restarts, visible and cancellable in the Registry. In each re-run Meeting a *named* Speaker is seeded from its own attributed segments embedded by the new model — the Operator's word about the voice, which ADR-0009 already puts outside the machine's reach — and those windows become its new exemplars. Pseudonymous Speakers are re-minted, so their numbers change. The Operator's Voiceprint is rebuilt by the channel rules alone (ADR-0029 as amended 2026-09-15), never from old attributions. A Meeting without Kept Audio keeps the old run's attributions.

## Considered options

Keep WeSpeaker and add PLDA/VBx (17 to 24 points of clustering error stay); Granola's two-model split (two self-hosted exports, and the masked path is unmeasured here); ReDimNet-B2 (about a point behind B3, no other difference). For the migration: re-embed only the old exemplars' stored sample offsets (rejected: uses the old model's cuts rather than the Operator's confirmation of whole clusters); a wipe with no re-run (rejected: a History nobody is recognized in); keep both vector spaces (rejected: recognition would silently depend on which model heard whom).

## Consequences

- Every installed build pins the export's URL and checksum; publishing it is an outward-facing act that happens once per model, under a written decision, never as a side effect of a build.
- Existing Voiceprints are gone on upgrade. Named Speakers come back as their Meetings are re-run; a named Speaker whose every Meeting lost its audio ends with no Voiceprint and a Registry note saying why.
- The M3 catalog's "reject, don't queue" for batch compute becomes "one queue, the fresh Meeting at the front"; only a Meeting already in line is refused.
- Thresholds (merge, match floor, margin) are re-derived on AMI dev and reported on test through the harness; until then Granola's 0.45 / 0.10 and the bake-off's 0.60 stand as named placeholders.
